# Observability: Prometheus, OpenTelemetry, Hubble

## Define the problem

A platform of many components fails in ways that no single log shows. Operators need metrics for trends and alerts, traces to follow a request across services, and network flow data to see who talks to whom. The problem is to build these three views for the two clusters so that a question such as why a service is slow can be answered from data rather than guesses.

## History and approaches

Monitoring began with host checks, then time-series metrics with pull-based collection (Prometheus) became the cloud-native default. Distributed tracing grew from research systems into open standards, and the OpenTelemetry project merged earlier efforts into one set of APIs, data formats and a collector. Network observability moved with eBPF: Hubble reads flow data from Cilium without sidecars.

## Current state

Prometheus scrapes targets found through service discovery, stores series, evaluates rules and sends alerts to a manager that groups and routes them. Applications and the platform expose metrics; dashboards are provisioned as code. The OpenTelemetry collector receives traces and metrics, processes them and exports to a backend. Hubble shows flows and policy verdicts per namespace and can export metrics. Together these give a chain: an alert from a metric, a trace of the slow request, and a flow view proving whether the network dropped traffic.

## Assessment: the good, the bad and the ugly

The three views complement each other and standard formats limit lock-in. The costs are storage and cardinality growth, sampling choices for traces, and the effort of instrumenting applications. Alert quality is a constant struggle: too many alerts train people to ignore them. The ugly part is gaps: components without metrics, or traces broken by a service that does not forward context headers.

## Applications beyond this domain

The same three-signal model applies to virtual machine fleets, serverless functions, data pipelines and mobile applications.

## Examples

A worked example: latency rises for one service; a metric alert fires, a trace shows time spent waiting for a database call, and Hubble shows retries after dropped connections caused by a new network policy. A failing example: an alert never fired because the target had been dropped from discovery after a label change; a rule that alerts on missing targets was added.

## Related problems and ideas

Related ideas include service level objectives, logging pipelines, continuous profiling and eBPF-based tracing. Alternatives to self-hosting are managed observability services.

## Active research

Active work includes OpenTelemetry stabilisation of signals, profiling as a fourth signal and cost control through sampling and aggregation (to verify).

## Future direction

Expect OpenTelemetry to become the common instrumentation layer and more correlation between signals in one tool.
