# Submit to a real subsystem list and address review

## Define the problem

The final test of kernel work is whether other maintainers accept it. That means sending a patch to a real mailing list, taking criticism in public, revising and resending. The problem is to go through a full review cycle on a genuine, small, correct change, and to document the process, with the understanding that acceptance is not guaranteed and not in the author's control.

## History and approaches

Kernel review has always been by mail, with maintainers owning subsystems and trees. Practices evolved: maintainer entry profiles, tags such as Reviewed-by and Acked-by, bots that test patches, and public archives that make history searchable. Newcomers get guidance from mentoring and the outreach programmes and from the staging area for drivers.

## Current state

The author chooses a modest target (a documentation fix, a small bug, a test) in a subsystem that welcomes it, reads past discussions, finds the maintainers and lists with the maintainer lookup script, and sends a series made with the standard tools. Replies are read in plain text and answered inline; revisions carry a version tag and a changelog below the line. Patience matters: replies can take weeks. The record of the cycle (links to the archive, versions sent, comments addressed) is the evidence.

## Assessment: the good, the bad and the ugly

The process teaches technical and social skills and produces real contributions. It is slow, sometimes blunt, and outcomes vary: a patch may be applied, rejected or ignored. The ugly part is reliance on others' time; the exercise is graded on process quality, not on acceptance.

## Applications beyond this domain

Open review by mail or forge is used across free software, standards bodies and scientific peer review. The habit of responding to each point and revising transfers everywhere.

## Examples

A worked example: a one-line fix to a documentation file receives a reviewer's comment about commit message wording; v2 is sent with a changelog, and the maintainer applies it. A failing example: a patch sent to the wrong list was ignored for two weeks until the right maintainer was copied.
