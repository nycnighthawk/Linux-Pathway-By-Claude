# Supply chain: cosign, SBOMs and admission checks

This lesson follows the topic synthesis `synth/LNX-314/LNX-314-T06.md`, which carries the sources, the history and the failure cases.

## What you will be able to do

- Explain why a tag is not an identity and a digest is, and what a signature adds on top of a digest.
- Sign and verify an image with cosign in keyless mode, and write the identity policy that makes the check meaningful.
- Generate and use an SBOM, and enforce signatures and attestations at admission.

## Two questions, two artefacts

| Question | Artefact |
|---|---|
| Where did this come from, and was it changed? | signature plus provenance attestation, bound to the image digest |
| What is inside it? | SBOM (SPDX or CycloneDX), matched against vulnerability data |

## Digests first

`app:1.4` is a pointer that anyone with push rights can move. `app@sha256:...` names content. Resolve tags to digests in CI or at admission, and verify the digest you run.

## Keyless signing in four parts

- **cosign** signs the digest and stores the signature next to the image in the registry.
- **Fulcio** issues a short-lived certificate for an identity proved by OIDC (a CI workflow, a person).
- **Rekor** is the public log that records the signature.
- **The policy** names the identity and the issuer you trust.

```bash
cosign sign registry.example/shop@sha256:<digest>
cosign verify registry.example/shop@sha256:<digest> \
  --certificate-identity "https://github.com/example/shop/.github/workflows/release.yml@refs/heads/main" \
  --certificate-oidc-issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com
```

"Any valid signature" is not a policy: anyone can sign with their own identity.

## SBOMs and attestations

An SBOM lists components found by scanning the image or observing the build. It has known gaps (static binaries, vendored sources), so treat it as an inventory with error. Its main use is answering which images contain a package at a version when a vulnerability is announced. Attach it as an in-toto attestation (`cosign attest`) so the cluster can check its content; BuildKit can emit SBOM and provenance attestations at build time.

## SLSA in one line

Level 1: provenance exists. Level 2: a hosted build service signs it. Level 3: the build is isolated so its steps cannot forge it.

## Enforce at admission

A Kyverno `verifyImages` rule, the Sigstore policy-controller (`ClusterImagePolicy`) or Ratify with Gatekeeper fetches signatures and attestations, checks the identity policy, and can rewrite the tag to the verified digest. Running Pods are not rechecked, so keep a background scan.

## Worked example: any signer passes

A policy accepted any valid Sigstore signature. An attacker with registry write access pushed a trojaned image under the same tag and signed it with their own email. The fix: a certificate identity and issuer pinned to the release workflow, deploys by digest, and write access limited to the release identity.
