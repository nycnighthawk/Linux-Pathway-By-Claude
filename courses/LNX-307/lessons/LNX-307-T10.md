# Production on-call

This lesson follows the topic synthesis `synth/LNX-307/LNX-307-T10.md`, which carries the sources and the history.

## What you will be able to do

- Prepare for a first shift: access, dashboards, runbooks, authority limits.
- Run the first minutes of an incident: acknowledge, assess, mitigate, preserve evidence, communicate, escalate.
- Write a blameless postmortem with a timeline, causes and actions that prevent, detect or mitigate.

## The rotation

A primary and a secondary, shifts with handover notes, an escalation tier above, and severity levels with response times (for example sev1 respond in 5 minutes, sev3 next business day). Alerts route by label ([T03](lesson:LNX-307-T03)) to the paging tool with escalation if unacknowledged. Healthy load: most pages actionable and few per shift; if not, the system is the problem.

Before the first shift test VPN, SSH, cloud console and hardware token from a phone and a laptop; know services, owners, dashboards ([T01](lesson:LNX-307-T01)) and runbooks; know what you may restart, roll back or fail over, and where the audited break-glass path is.

## First minutes

1. Acknowledge and read the alert and its runbook link.
2. Assess impact from the user-facing view (RED). Declare an incident if warranted and name a commander; a larger incident also has a scribe and a communications lead.
3. **Mitigate first, diagnose second**: roll back, fail over, shed load, flip a feature flag. But **collect evidence first**: `journalctl -b > file`, `ps auxf`, `free`, `ss -s`, pressure files, `dmesg -T`, thread or heap dump, and check for OOM kills ([T08](lesson:LNX-307-T08)).
4. Follow the method ([T01](lesson:LNX-307-T01), [T02](lesson:LNX-307-T02), [T04](lesson:LNX-307-T04), [T05](lesson:LNX-307-T05)); write timestamped notes.
5. Update stakeholders on a schedule (every 30 minutes), plainly.
6. Escalate early: no hypothesis after 15 to 30 minutes, growing impact or unknown territory.
7. Verify recovery in user-facing signals and watch for relapse.

Habits: use `tmux`, keep read-only commands first, copy before editing, undo packages with `dnf history undo`, never run destructive commands without a second pair of eyes.

## Blameless postmortem

Summary and impact; UTC timeline; root and contributing causes; what went well; what went badly; where we got lucky; action items with owner and date, each tagged prevent, detect or mitigate. Feed results back: fix the runbook ([T01](lesson:LNX-307-T01)), tune alerts ([T03](lesson:LNX-307-T03)), add a drill ([T07](lesson:LNX-307-T07)), add a missing log field or metric. Look at how the system let a person make the error, not who made it.

## Worked example

Page at 02:10: high error ratio on checkout. The runbook points to a decision tree. Errors began at 02:05 when a deploy finished. The responder takes a journal export and a thread dump from one failing instance, rolls back, watches errors drop within three minutes, posts a summary and opens a ticket. A bad example: a restart at 03:00 cleared a memory leak without evidence; the OOM kill recurred twice the next week and the diagnosis took longer.
